Hall of fame

What this is

This started life as rednuht's Genetic Cars, a 2011 page that was itself a take on BoxCar2D. The idea was lovely and simple: throw random two wheeled shapes at a hill, keep the ones that get furthest, breed them, and watch a car appear out of nothing. That code sat untouched from 2013, running on a Flash port of Box2D.

BoxCar3D is a rebuild of it. Same idea, three changes. Cars now run in three dimensions on Box3D, which is Erin Catto's newer engine and a direct descendant of the Box2D the original used. The road banks from side to side, so a car can roll off the edge instead of only ever getting stuck. And every car carries a small neural network that actually drives it.

That last one is the real difference. In the original a wheel spun at one fixed speed forever, so a car was a shape and nothing else. Here the network reads six things about how the car is sitting and moving, and sets the speed of each wheel from them. Its weights are genes like any other, so the search is over bodies and drivers at the same time, and you can watch both change in the Driver panel.

A car scores by how far it gets plus a bonus for average speed. It dies when it stops earning ground, and the whole generation ends early once nobody is making progress, so one crawler cannot hold everyone up. The terrain gets steeper the further you go.

The flat mode is still here under 2D. It is the original problem, rebuilt on planck.js, and it is a good place to watch the search when the 3D camera is too busy. Each mode keeps its own records, since they are different problems on different scales.

Physics by Box3D via box3d-wasm, and planck.js in the flat mode. Rendering by three.js. Credit for the original goes to rednuht.
